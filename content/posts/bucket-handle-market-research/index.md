## Research
The first step to any engineering product is research. After all, when Newton said "Dwarves standing on the shoulders of giants can see further than the giants" it was true, innovation produces a product faster than invention. By looking at the limited other options on the market I was able to evaluate several different design approaches and find my own unique idea. Others seemed too complicated with a screw together design or requiring the user to slide multiple pieces together. These either increased the final part count beyond what I thought was justified for the extra security or required more exact tolerances that I thought was necessary in this kind of product. I also found features that I thought were highly worth including such as contouring the handle for the comfort of fingers and also found the general dimensions that I should use. After this research I decided to focus my design on the following criteria:

1. Ergonomics
  - Very important as if it is uncomfortable there is no reason to use the product over just the wire handle
2. Durability
  - If it breaks then it is no better than the original handle and I wanted my product to be quantitatively better.
3. Cost
  - I had very few funds to make my design, I wanted to use materials and manufacturing methods that I already had. This gave me access to using my 3d printer and a variety of materials.
4. Ease of Maintenance
  - I didn't want to make something that would eventually get so disgusting that people wouldn't want to use it even with gloves on.

According to this list I started planning designs that would address each feature in order. The design would be a single piece of tpu printed to snap onto the wire with contours for fingers. I also planned to print it vertically and take advantage of a special mode of printing that left the ceiling and floor open while using a rectangular style infill to provide support.
