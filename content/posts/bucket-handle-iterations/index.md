## Design Revision 1
I started by out modelling the end profile and extruding the shape up, then cut the grips using a perpendicular plane, and finally shelled the entire thing. After adding some fillets and defining the dimensions so the model was stable and defined I was ready for the first test print. For the first test I relied on the rectilinear infill pattern generated by Simplify3d but this turned out to be a mistake as the pattern generated meant that the handle was very strong in the axis directions but folded easily when compacted in any other direction. This led to the material splitting at the layer lines while under deformation. 

For the next trial I tried to increase the density of the infill and thicken the lines hoping that would fix the deformation issue while also printing at a higher temperature with a lower fan cooling speed to increase layer adhesion. This resulted in a handle that handled off axis deformation acceptably but was rock hard in the direction of normal load. Due to the increased temperature there were also more thin strands left in the cavities between the infill lines and it just looked messy.

These models were valuable in that they let me fiddle with the tolerances of the snap fit that I had designed. Initially the snap portion ran the entire length of the handle but this made the handle difficult to snap onto the wire. Once it was on then it wouldn't come off but it seemed like too much work and it wouldn't accept curved handles very well. I next tried splitting the snap portion into three sections, one on either end and one in the center while only leaving ~0.2mm of a gap between the wire's nominal diameter and the inside wall of the snap portion. This turned out to be too tight of a tolerance for the material and printing temperature and led to a snap that wouldn't close correctly and deformed the material while also allowing the grip to fall off easily. Rather than altering the printing profile to more closely match the physical result, I increased the tolerance of the design to around ~0.5mm of a gap but made the snap tighter. ## Design Revision 2
This worked reasonably but the infill pattern still left a lot to be desired. I decided therefore to switch to designing in a support structure reminiscent of bicycle spokes. The idea being that the material is not very good at compression but it fantastic under tension, I wouldn't be able to pretension the 'spokes' at all but I figured it should still resist deformation fairly well. The design I ended up with after a few trial fiddling with the size of the spokes is shown below. 
{{ figure(src="spokes.png", alt="Second major revision of the handle with bicycle spokes.") }}
By varying the width of the spokes I could adjust how much deformation the handle experienced under load. I decided to print a variety of options and chose the one that deformed under a full 5 gallon bucket of water so the grip almost touched the center structure but not quite. This helped spread the load on the finger and was the most comfortable of the options.

## Personalization
The basic structure of the grip done I set about making them fun for my step dad as I knew he would appreciate a bit of humor. To that end, I brainstormed several different phrases to embed into the sides. We ended up with the following:

- I wish it was a beer bucket
- Don't kick the bucket
- Shit's heavy!
- Rick's Bucket

After coming up with these phrases I proceeded to the testing phases using the prototypes I had already printed.
